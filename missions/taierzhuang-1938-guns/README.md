# Battle of Taierzhuang, II: the guns at the canal, 30 March – 1 April 1938

The second part of the Battle of Taierzhuang. The Japanese Seya Detachment holds most of the
walled town on the Grand Canal's north bank, its artillery massed round Liujiahu and Sanlizhuang
to the north. From the hills south of the canal the Chinese 10th Artillery Regiment's two German
15 cm sFH 18/32L howitzers, which outrange every Japanese gun there, open on the Japanese
batteries from 31 March. The replay follows the artillery battle through the Japanese reports,
from the night attacks of 30 March to the bombardments of 1 April. The Chinese side is the
friendly force. The defence and fall of Tengxian is the first part.

This folder is the package (`operation.json`, `events.jsonl`, `presentations/default.json`) and
its generator, `build/`. Regenerate with `python3 build/taierzhuang_guns.py`, then run
`python3 ../../verify.py`.

## Method

- **Clock.** UTC+8, which the Japanese reports name (「時刻ハ西部標準時トス」); the 63rd
  Infantry's daily sunrise and sunset fit it to within three minutes. Operation zero is 01:30 on
  30 March, as the Chinese night attack sets out from the station.
- **The main records** are Japanese, read page by page from the archive's images: the 63rd
  Infantry's battle report for Taierzhuang, the 10th Infantry's report No. 13, and the battle
  report of the Type 96 15 cm company (臨SAS戦詳第一號), with their sketch maps. The postwar
  manuscript of 大橋武夫, a staff officer of the heavy artillery, gives the Japanese gunners'
  verdict on the duel. No Chinese battle report of the 7th or 10th Artillery was found; the
  Chinese side comes from 萨沙's account of the sFH 18 in China and Wikipedia.
- **The first 15 cm rounds.** Chinese accounts have the 15 cm guns duelling from 27 March. The
  Japanese reports first record rounds of the 15 cm class at 15:46 on 31 March, knocking out a
  Type 4 howitzer of the 3rd Heavy Company; the replay follows them (first-15cm-fire).
- **Where the guns stood** is not in any record. The Japanese saw the rounds come from the
  Xiongshan direction and never found the guns. The replay puts them about 13 km south of the
  Japanese gun line, beyond the Type 96's reach and within their own of Liujiahu, and gives the
  position a 3 km bound (sfh-position).
- **Range fans.** Each battery's reach is a MIL-STD-2525D sector range fan opening 90° towards
  the other side's guns: the sFH 18/32L's 15,000 m, the Type 96's 11,900 m, the Type 4's
  8,800 m, the Type 38 field gun's 11,600 m. No range is recorded for the Chinese field guns, so
  they have none.
- **Fire missions** are drawn as rounds flying from a battery to its target at the times the
  reports give, at a notional 280 m/s; their tracks show who fired on whom, not ballistics.
- **The town of 1938** stood on the canal's north bank; the reach through it was straightened in
  1959 and the old channel is today's 月河, drawn by the basemap. The branch railway from
  Zaozhuang, taken up in 1945, is drawn on the sketch maps' line within about 300 m; the
  station's site is the Li Zongren museum, rebuilt there in 1995.
- **On screen** names and quotations are given in English and pinyin; the viewer cannot yet
  draw Chinese or Japanese characters. The ledger keeps the originals.
- **Build checks.** `build/taierzhuang_guns.py` fails if a battery is not standing where its
  range fan is drawn; if a round flies further than its gun's reach; if the 15 cm position lies
  within the Type 96's reach or beyond its own of Liujiahu and Sanlizhuang; if troops on foot
  pass 7 km/h or the train 25 km/h; or if an estimate in `build/evidence/` lies outside its
  records without saying why.
