# John Brown's raid on Harpers Ferry, 16–18 October 1859

John Brown and eighteen men of the Provisional Army of the United States of America march from
the Kennedy farm in Maryland, cut the telegraph, take the federal armory, arsenal and rifle works
at Harpers Ferry and seize hostages, to free and arm the enslaved. The B&O express they hold and then let go carries the news east;
by Tuesday morning militia from Virginia and Maryland, and marines brought by the railroad, have
them shut in the armory's fire-engine house, which the marines storm. The replay is told from the
raiders' side: they are the friendly force.

This folder is the package (`operation.json`, `events.jsonl`, `presentations/default.json`) and
its generator, `build/`. Regenerate with `python3 build/harpers_ferry.py`, then run
`python3 ../../verify.py`.

## Method

- **Clock.** Harpers Ferry local mean time, UTC−5:10:55. In 1859 each town kept its own time;
  the records' times are as their writers gave them, and the minutes between the towns along the
  railroad (Monocacy 1.4 min, Baltimore 4.4 min ahead) are finer than the records and are not
  corrected. Operation zero is 14:30 on Sunday 16 October, the afternoon Brown gave his orders; the council
  that morning is a moment of the ledger before it. The viewer's
  "Local" clock follows today's zone boundaries, whose 1859 rule for this place is New York local
  mean time, about fifteen minutes ahead of Harpers Ferry's; each source declares the Harpers
  Ferry clock, on which its events are shown.
- **Names.** The friendly force is the Provisional Army of the United States of America. Its
  ranks and posts are those of Anderson's eleven orders of Sunday afternoon: Brown
  Commander-in-Chief, Kagi Adjutant General, and the captains and lieutenants as the orders call
  them; each man is placed under the post the orders gave him. Ages are those of the "In Memoriam" plaque at the Kennedy farm.
- **1859, not today.** The town is not today's: the armory's yard along the Potomac lies under the
  B&O's raised line of 1892–94, the engine house was moved away in 1891 and stands now about
  150 ft from its site, and both bridges are gone. The replay puts the places where they stood:
  - the engine house and watch-house at the B&O's 1895 obelisk (OSM node 356535602), which marks
    the original site; the armory gate on the street at its end, "about 60 yards" from the bridge
    (Starry);
  - the B&O's covered bridge of 1836–37 on the line of the stone piers at the Point (OSM way
    549095499), the line Villard calls the "Site of Old Bridge by which Brown and his men
    entered", not the 1894 bridge upstream; the station and the Wager House at its town end;
  - the Shenandoah bridge on the line of the later piers at the Point, an assumption;
  - the arsenal at the outlined ruins of Arsenal Square, and Hall's rifle works at the ruins on
    Lower Hall Island, about half a mile up the Shenandoah, as Starry puts it;
  - the railroad east of the bridge round the foot of Maryland Heights and round Point of Rocks
    beside the canal, without the tunnels of 1894 and the 1860s; beyond Point of Rocks the Old
    Main Line to Monocacy (Frederick Junction, not today's Monocacy station) and Baltimore.
  The map and the ground the viewer draws are today's: the railroad's embankment over the armory
  site and the 1894 bridge are not of 1859.
- **The march.** From the farm (the National Historic Landmark nomination's point) by the lane,
  Chestnut Grove Road and the river road down the Maryland bank, 7.65 km to the bridge. Villard
  has the Jefferson Guards come down to the bridge "over the same road over which the raiders had
  come", from above the town, so the march does not go by Sandy Hook. It leaves at eight
  (Anderson) and reaches the bridge at half past ten, a walk of about 3 km/h; Cook and Tidd walk
  four minutes ahead, Kagi and Stevens a minute ahead of the wagon, the others in pairs behind.
- **The country.** Stevens's party goes out through Bolivar by the Charlestown pike to Beall-Air
  and comes back by Allstadt's house on the pike, at the times in the ledger; the pike's line is
  approximate, within 300 m.
- **The trains.** The express comes in from the west along the Virginia bank at 1:25, backs off
  the bridge, stands at the station until six, follows Brown over the bridge at a walk, and runs
  east at 23 mph: Monocacy at 7:05 (telegram), standing until eight (the Baltimore Patriot's
  "passed the Monocacy at eight"), Ellicott's Mills by eleven, Baltimore before noon. The troop
  trains follow the B&O's telegrams: the marines from Washington by the 3:30 train to Relay, then
  with the Baltimore companies, at Monocacy at 8:10 and Sandy Hook before eleven; Lee's special
  from Relay soon after.
- **The militia.** The Jefferson Guards and Botts's company from Charlestown by train to the
  heights; the Guards cross the Potomac by boat above the town and come down the Maryland bank to
  the bridge (Villard); the Martinsburg company from the west to the upper end of the armory
  yard; Frederick's three companies from Monocacy to Sandy Hook and over the bridge after
  sunset. Their approaches outside the town are reconstructed within 1.5 km.
- **The telegraph.** Brown's fourth order sends Cook and Tidd ahead to cut the wires on the
  Maryland side along the railroad, and on the Virginia side once the town is taken. No source
  gives where or when; the ledger's estimate places the cuts. The line east works again by
  midnight on Monday, when Smith gives it "up to reporters".
- **Small moves.** A few moves are placed so the replay shows what the sources describe without
  saying where: Green bringing Washington's carriage round from the outbuildings; Tidd and Cook
  loading at the house and at the cabin across the road, where the pikes were kept; Brown carried
  to the paymaster's office after the assault (Greene); and the Baltimore companies coming into
  the town on Tuesday morning (Villard). Each stays within its site's bound.
- **Who is shown.** Every raider, the militia companies, the marines, Lee and Stuart, and the
  express. The hostages and the enslaved men brought from Beall-Air and Allstadt's are in the
  briefings, not on the map, as are the townspeople killed: Heyward Shepherd, Thomas Boerley,
  George W. Turner and the mayor, Fontaine Beckham.
- **Build checks.** `build/harpers_ferry.py` fails if anyone on foot moves faster than 7 km/h, a
  wagon 12 km/h or a train 25 mph, the B&O's own limit of 1859; if the express is not at Monocacy
  at 7:05, or Kagi, Newby or Leeman dies away from where the sources put him; or if an estimate in
  `build/evidence/` lies outside its records without saying why.

## Conflicts carried

The sources' accounts are kept in `build/evidence/`, one module per part of the story; the
replay's estimates are the dispute entries' "Replay estimate" column. The replay shows a viewer
only a one-line brief where a dispute changes what is shown.
